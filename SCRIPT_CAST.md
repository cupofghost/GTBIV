# GTB IV: San Chaos — Cast, Locations & State (script pass)

> Companion to `SCRIPT.md` and `SCRIPT_STRANDS.md`. Character sheets for
> everyone new, location sheets for everywhere new, the state flags the script
> assumes, and the engineering notes.

## 1. New character sheets — principals

### Desmond Voss
```
NAME: Desmond Voss                ROLE: ally (the strand's heart)
One-line: A permit clerk in his late fifties who owns one extraordinary shirt
  and bought it, twenty-two years ago, with his daughter's tuition money.
Voice: Precise, courteous, unhurried. Full sentences with the punctuation
  audible. Never raises his voice and never softens a fact. Talks about
  fabric the way everyone else in this game talks about cars, and is not
  self-conscious about it.
  Sample: "It fits a man named Errol Halloran's idea of me, from a fitting
  room in nineteen ninety-eight. It's never going to fit anybody else."
Wants: Nothing, visibly. What he's actually doing is keeping one bad morning
  in 1998 from having been for nothing, and the shirt is how he does it.
Relationship to Turbo: The one person in the game who has made Turbo's exact
  mistake and can describe it from the far side. Neither of them ever says
  this out loud — it's the reason the strand works and stating it would kill it.
First appearance: voss_first_sighting, Chapter 3.
Arc / payoff: refuses Turbo → is robbed by Turbo → doesn't call the police →
  tells him what the shirt cost → lends it to him for court → gives it to him.
  He is the only character who gives Turbo something without a condition.
Recurring details: works the same window for twenty-six years; Thursday to
  Monday the shirt is at Ilse's; his daughter is forty-one, lives ninety
  minutes away, doesn't call, and he mentions this exactly once.
```

### Ilse Trang
```
NAME: Ilse Trang                  ROLE: ally / gatekeeper
One-line: Owns Trang's Fine Care and has personally kept the Meridian alive
  for eleven of its twenty-two years.
Voice: Short. Declarative. Never explains twice. The only character who tells
  Turbo useful things before he needs them.
  Sample: "Cold. Always cold. Warm water sets a stain like it's setting
  concrete, and everyone does it warm, and then they bring it to me."
Wants: For people to stop ruining garments she can't replace.
Relationship to Turbo: calls Voss on him the night of the theft, then teaches
  him how not to do it again — in that order, without contradiction.
First appearance: trang_introduction, Chapter 3.
Arc / payoff: rebuilds the Meridian from the Halloran bolt. If Turbo sells
  it, she's the one who tells him it's in Halberstam's window, and she does
  not shame him, which lands harder than shaming would.
Recurring details: knows every garment on the block by touch; the shop is
  always technically closed and the door is always open.
```

### Ambrose Kell
```
NAME: Ambrose Kell                ROLE: temptation (Ch1) / straight answer (Ch3)
One-line: The shirtmaker at VESTRY who sells Turbo an $800 shirt on day one
  and is completely honest with him about it both times.
Voice: Trade-craft precision. Not a salesman — a man describing his own work
  accurately, which is more persuasive than selling would be.
  Sample: "A Meridian isn't a size, it's a man's measurements from nineteen
  ninety-something. Somebody else's shoulders."
Wants: To sell shirts he stands behind. That's the whole character.
Relationship to Turbo: sold him the thing that ruined his day one. Feels no
  guilt and offers no refund, and is not written as a villain for either.
First appearance: vestry_offer, Chapter 1.
Recurring details: doesn't put prices on anything; prices a shirt after
  looking at the man; will not buy back what he sells.
```

### Claudia Renwick
```
NAME: Claudia Renwick             ROLE: obstacle (the system)
One-line: Deb's family-law attorney — devastatingly polite, entirely
  competent, and never once unfair.
Voice: Answers exactly what she's asked and nothing more. Warns Turbo about
  his own interests unprompted, which is somehow worse than hostility.
  Sample: "You shouldn't talk to me without counsel." / "I know. That's why
  I said it."
Wants: Her client's arrears collected. She has no personal stake and this is
  what makes her frightening.
Relationship to Turbo: opposing counsel. She is the first person in the game
  to tell him the truth about his situation and she does it for free.
First appearance: deb_arrears / deb_served, Chapter 2.
Recurring details: always has the folder; never raises the number without
  saying who decided it; picked the $5,600 herself and admits it.
```

### The Server
```
NAME: credited only as THE SERVER   ROLE: pressure (roads B/C)
One-line: A man in a windbreaker whose entire job is handing Turbo an
  envelope, and who will do it for as long as it takes.
Voice: Flat, procedural, unfailingly polite. Never threatens. Never hurries.
  Sample: "You've been served. Hearing's on the eleventh, nine a.m.,
  Department Four. It's all in there, including the new number."
Wants: To complete service.
Relationship to Turbo: none. That's the horror of him.
First appearance: deb_served, Chapter 2, roads B/C only.
Recurring details: **never runs.** If the player flees, he simply appears
  somewhere else later, at walking pace, still holding the envelope. He is
  the only pursuer in the game that a car does not solve.
```

### Judge Aurelio Bask
```
NAME: Judge Aurelio Bask          ROLE: the verdict
One-line: A family-court judge with a long morning list who has heard every
  version of Turbo's story and is neither moved nor cruel.
Voice: Efficient, bored, occasionally and unexpectedly kind. Explains the
  rules to unrepresented parties because it saves him time later.
  Sample: "Do you want to say anything? You don't have to. Plenty of people
  don't and it works out about the same."
Wants: To get through the list.
First appearance: the_hearing, Chapter 5.
Recurring details: refuses to let Cornelius speak, and is right to; his
  "you're not the worst thing on it" is the most generous line available in
  the contempt ending.
```

### Hollis Prine
```
NAME: Hollis Prine                ROLE: antagonist (and not a villain)
One-line: A developer who bought the Wildcats Field parcel legally at an
  auction nobody attended, and who needs one true sentence from an alum to
  close.
Voice: Patient, plain, occasionally generous. Refuses to be cast as the bad
  guy and says so out loud once. Every argument he makes is correct, which
  is the entire problem with him.
  Sample: "That's not devotion, that's a man who can't stop."
Wants: The parcel, cleanly, on schedule. No malice in any direction.
Relationship to Turbo: offers him $2,200 for two true paragraphs.
First appearance: prine_stakes, Chapter 4.
Arc / payoff: if Turbo signs, Prine wins and is gracious about it. If Turbo
  runs Homecoming, Prine loses a filing cycle and is equally gracious, and
  tells Turbo exactly what a season cost him.
Recurring details: photographs everything; has walked the parcel eleven
  times; never once raises his offer, because the offer was fair the first
  time.
```

### Ruth Kessler
```
NAME: Ruth Kessler                ROLE: ally / the price of loyalty
One-line: Runs the harbour weigh station, pays fairly, and writes down

Voice: Three rules, said in the same order to everybody. Completely without
```

## 5. Engineering notes

Ranked by distance from what already ships. Flagged, not assumed —
`STORY_BIBLE.md` §12 and `HANDOFF.md` §8's rule.

1. **Day counter (1..11).** *Low.* The day/night cycle already exists; this is
   an integer that increments on rollover and a HUD line. It replaces the $800
   clock as the chapter pressure and everything else in this script hangs off it.
   Build this first — nothing else works without it.
2. **Branch flags + the three Ch1 exits.** *Low.* Three cutscene entries and one
   enum on `G.story`. `deb_refusal` needs a decline path on the existing payoff
   prompt; `deb_empty_handed` needs a "player is broke and bought the Kell"
   predicate. Both are conditions on a trigger that already fires.
3. **VESTRY as a shop interior.** *Low–moderate.* If there's no shop-interior
   pattern, this can be a proximity trigger + prompt at a storefront with no
   interior at all, and the cutscenes carry it. Do not build an interior system
   for one shirt.
4. **A worn-shirt slot on the player mesh.** *Moderate.* `js/person.js` already
   takes `outfit.shirt.color` / `.tex` per `CHARACTERS.md` §3, so "which shirt is
   Turbo wearing" is a spec swap on an existing field, not new rig work. Three
   values: default, Kell, Meridian. The Meridian should read as visibly better
   at a glance and visibly slightly wrong on him in `voss_fit` — a one-frame
   sleeve-length difference is enough; nobody needs tailoring simulation.
5. **The conversation node (M8 / Danny's shed).** *Low.* Danny's shed already
   works this way. Trang's is the same thing with a different pool.
6. **Delicate Cycle.** *Low.* The shipped heist loop with the safe-crack step
   re-skinned as a rail search. No guards. If the stealth layer needs a body,
   one non-hostile presser.
7. **THE SERVER.** *Moderate, and the one genuinely new AI shape.* A pursuer
   with no combat, no aggro, and no chase — he walks, he despawns when lost, and
   he respawns ahead of the player somewhere else the next day. Structurally a
   waypoint-following ped with a proximity cutscene, not a hostile. Do **not**
   route him through the cop/jock hostility code; he must never be punchable in
   a way that has consequences, and ideally not punchable at all.
8. **The three offers as persistent, reversible choices.** *Low.* Prine's
   affidavit, Hardcastle's page, Donna's napkin, and Mo's ticket are all one
   boolean each, and three of the four are irreversible by design. Only the pawn
   ticket stays live to the gavel, and that difference is doing real work — keep
   it.
9. **`the_hearing`'s conditional shots.** *Moderate.* The scene is twelve shots
   with three of them gated on flags (shot 4 on `ledgerSeen`, shot 8 on road C,
   shot 11 on `church.visits >= 2`). The shipped cutscene system is a shot list
   with durations; per-shot conditions are a new field on the shot record. Small,
   but it is a change to the cutscene data shape — worth naming out loud before
   someone tries to fake it with three separate cutscene entries.
10. **Homecoming.** *Low.* Three delivery legs into one shipped Turbo Bowl run.
    FB4 already built the run; this reuses it with occupied stands and different
    framing. No new mode.

**Not needed, deliberately:** no new combat, no new vehicle, no new weapon, no
child model, no courtroom NPC crowd beyond seated extras, no flashback system
(1984 is told, never shown), and no dialogue-tree UI — every choice in this
script is a two-option prompt of the kind the game already renders.
